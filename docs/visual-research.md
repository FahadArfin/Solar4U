# Solar4U experience review — 7 September 2026

The original home page was captured in this run. It used tightly packed navigation, muted blue surfaces, a large empty hero, and hardcoded market totals (18,420 products, 126 daily drops, 1.2M records). The displayed price sparklines were manufactured from placeholder/high/current prices rather than dated observations. These were credibility problems as well as visual problems.

The learning flow mixed 34 beginner and professional chapters, placed manuals ahead of fundamentals, counted opened pages rather than knowledge checks, and did not retain progress after reload. Its image optimizer also threw an exception when the asset binding was absent during local preview. The planner first step consumed most of the viewport before useful site interactions were available. Its geometry accuracy issues are covered in the solar audit.

New direction: a sunlit architectural fieldbook, with warm paper, forest green, solar yellow, an original house model, short outcome-based navigation and clear source/assumption labels. Visual inspiration comes from wayfinding and modularity, not copied layouts or assets.

- [Energy Park by Outpost](https://outpost.design/work/energy-park/): Awwwards Site of the Day / Developer Award, FWA Site of the Day, Lovie Best UX Silver. Solution-led copy, transit-inspired wayfinding and full mobile functionality.
- [Energy Park design case study](https://www.awwwards.com/energy-park-behind-the-scenes-case-study.html).
- [Rickman Architecture + Design](https://radga.com/): spatial imagery and restrained type hierarchy; recognition in the [Awwwards grid design archive](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/dynamic-grid-layout-examples/).

Original generated imagery: `solar-home.png`, `learning-roof-mounting.png`, `learning-system-components.png`. These are conceptual illustrations, not surveyed property images, installation drawings, or representations of real equipment models. Existing social artwork is retained. Source photographs from award references were not reused.

Current-run UI evidence lives in the local `qa/revamp` folder (excluded from the public repository). Screenshots establish layout/visible behavior, not full accessibility compliance. Keyboard controls, mobile layout, native WebMCP and production routes are checked separately.
